AI can now see, read, and speak. The question that drives me is: who is it actually built for?

My research interest lies at the intersection of Human-Computer Interaction, Human-Centered AI, Computer Vision, and Natural Language Processing. I'm most interested in how perception and generative models can be designed to support people that mainstream technology often leaves out.

My undergraduate research at KUET focused on assistive and health technology. I built a smart-spectacle system that helps visually impaired people navigate and read, a unified vision platform for recognizing objects, currency, and faces, and a smartphone app that estimates blood glucose and hemoglobin non-invasively from fingertip video. All three were published at IEEE conferences. At BRAC University I'm now working on AI-assisted video creation for people who stutter and long-video understanding with vision-language models, and I recently built a GNN–BERT model that fuses audio graphs and text for music understanding.

I spent two years as a software engineer in fintech, working on the backend of Nagad, one of Bangladesh's largest mobile financial services. That experience taught me how much system design decisions affect real users at scale, and I bring that view to my research.

I'm also a competitive programmer: a Codeforces Specialist (max rating 1442) with 1100+ problems solved across online judges. Contest programming built the algorithmic thinking and problem-solving habits I rely on in both research and engineering. More below ↓

An Assistive Device for the Visually Impaired for Enhanced Navigation and Reading

13th International Conference on Electrical and Computer Engineering (ICECE), 2024

A portable embedded smart-spectacle system that gives visually impaired people mobility and reading support. It handles real-time obstacle avoidance, detects falls and alerts caregivers automatically, and reads text aloud through an OCR pipeline with image preprocessing and text-to-speech. Automated Object Identification for the Visually Impaired

27th International Conference on Computer and Information Technology (ICCIT), 2024

A single automated assistive platform that combines object detection, currency recognition, and face recognition. It uses YOLOv5 for detection and ResNet-50 for classification, trained on curated datasets, with separate modules that support navigation, financial transactions, and social interaction. Current Research

HCI · Marginalized Community · Manuscript under review

AI-Assisted Video Creation for People Who Stutter

A qualitative HCI study with eight Bangladeshi people who stutter, combining an onboarding workshop with semi-structured interviews and reflexive thematic analysis, to explore how they create videos and use AI to edit their speech.

We examined how the purpose of a video, its audience, and personal preference influenced whether participants kept or edited out their stuttering, and found that they want AI tools that lower editing effort while letting creators stay in control of how their voice is represented.
